The Competitive Moat

Apple and Google will never give web apps deep system access because:

  • It undermines their native app stores (30% revenue cut)
  • It undermines developer lock-in to Swift/Kotlin
  • It enables cross-platform apps that reduce platform switching cost
  • Their business models depend on the native app ecosystem

AIPhone OS has no native app store to protect. Giving web apps deep access IS the product.

Historical Context

Firefox OS (2012-2015)

Mozilla built a web-only phone OS with HTML5 apps and device capability APIs. It reached 12 smartphones across 24 countries by 2014, then was discontinued.

It failed for multiple reasons: web apps had no more capability than browser tabs, the app ecosystem was too thin, hardware distribution was limited to low-end devices, and iOS/Android were too entrenched.

Our key difference: TWAs get more capability than native apps — not less. The AI generation model means the ecosystem bootstraps from day one. And we start with enthusiast hardware (Pixel), not bottom-tier devices.

ChromeOS / Googlebook (2011-2026)

Google proved a web-centric OS can work (10%+ US laptop market). In June 2024, ChromeOS adopted parts of the Android stack. In May 2026, Google launched Googlebook with Gemini at the center.

Lesson: AI as central OS interaction layer is commercially validated. But Google preserves the existing app ecosystem — they won't break the app store model. We can.

KaiOS

HTML5-based app OS for smart feature phones. Proved web-app OS architecture works on real constrained hardware. We target high-capability smartphones and tablets, not feature phones.

Market Reality

As of April 2026: Android 67.35%, iOS 32.55%, everything else near zero (StatCounter).

The opportunity is enormous. The ecosystem barrier is equally enormous. That's why we start with developer/enthusiast users on Pixel hardware, not mass-market.
